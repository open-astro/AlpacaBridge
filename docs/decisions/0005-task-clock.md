# Task clock

Status: accepted

## Context

Every driver timer waits on the wall clock, and so do its tests. At f27c3e1, production code under `AlpacaCore/src/` and `AlpacaCore/include/` has 125 raw sleep lines across 34 files, and the tests have 149 lines that mention `sleep_for` (143 calls; the other 6 are comments), 57 of them in `AlpacaCore/tests/test_skywatcher_async.cpp`. A test that sleeps to wait for a driver timer is slow when the sleep is generous and flaky when it is not (#535). The SkyWatcher, Celestron and SynScan telescope drivers each carry a `task_wait_for` for their cancellable task waits, and SkyWatcher also sleeps directly in its poll, settle and autohome paths. Of the shared wait and deadline helpers, only `util::StreamLinkHealth` (`AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/link_health.h`) and the client-silence watchdog's `note_client_activity` and `stop_motion_if_client_silent` (`AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/telescope_driver.h`) take a `now` parameter today. `util::ConsecutiveSettle` (`AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/poll_settle.h`) is clock-free by design, since its poll count is the elapsed time, and `PolledLinkHealth` counts failures.

## Decision

Every driver wait and deadline goes through an injected `TaskClock` with `now()`, a cancellable `wait_for` over the caller's mutex, condition variable and predicate, and a plain `sleep_for`. It is real by default and fake in tests. The real adapter wraps `std::chrono::steady_clock` and `std::condition_variable::wait_for`. The fake holds virtual time: `advance()` wakes every waiter whose deadline has passed, and `wait_for_waiters(n)` is the rendezvous that replaces sleep-and-hope in tests.

The fake's `advance()` takes each due waiter's mutex before notifying it, so a wakeup cannot fall between the waiter's predicate check and its block. The test thread that calls `advance()` holds no driver lock.

Once a driver is on the clock, every wait in it goes through the clock: the task waits, the rate-verify window, and the raw settle and poll sleeps. A test that sleeps to wait for a driver timer on such a driver is a review-blocking regression. `StreamLinkHealth` callers take `now()` from the clock; `ConsecutiveSettle` stays clock-free; `PolledLinkHealth` holds no clock and counts failures, and its caller passes `now()` on the call that latches a fault, so the fault's age can be read against the staleness bound of [decision 0009](0009-link-loss-and-relink-policy.md) point 2(b).

Pointing time and clock-step detection stay on the seams of [decision 0001](0001-skywatcher-pointing-clock.md). This clock is for waits and deadlines, never for the LST or UTCDate value; [decision 0014](0014-utc-offset.md) splits the two: the UTC value stays on `system_clock`, and only the host time elapsed since the write reads this clock.

A protocol wrapper stays on the real clock for wire timing (rule 6). The kernel read timeout inside the same poll loop is real time, and `FakeTaskClock::sleep_for` blocks until `advance()`, so a fake clock in a wire loop would stall every protocol command in a test while the read stays real.

## Rules

The rules a change is checked against. Each names where it applies and what checks it; the reasoning is in the Decision above.

1. In a driver whose Adoption row is adopted, every wait, sleep and deadline reads the injected `TaskClock`. Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_driver.cpp`. Checked by: review; the roster by `scripts/check_docs_drift.py` check 23.
2. A test of such a driver waits for a driver timer through the fake clock (`advance()`, `wait_for_waiters()`), never with `sleep_for`. Scope: `AlpacaCore/tests/test_*.cpp`. Checked by: review.
3. The task clock never supplies a UTC or LST value. An elapsed time measured on it may be added to a UTC value that came from elsewhere, because the clock cannot step: the time since a UTCDate write ([decision 0014](0014-utc-offset.md)), and the Celestron CGX GOTO correction lead (the time the previous correction of the same slew took from aiming to tracking, added to the UTC the next correction aims at). Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_driver.cpp`. Checked by: review.
4. `StreamLinkHealth` callers pass `now()` from the clock, and so does a `PolledLinkHealth` caller that reads `fault_stale()`; neither type holds a clock, and `ConsecutiveSettle` takes none. Scope: `AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/*.h`, `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/**`. Checked by: review.
5. The fake's `advance()` takes each due waiter's mutex before it notifies it. Scope: `AlpacaCore/tests/fake_task_clock.h`. Checked by: `AlpacaCore/tests/test_task_clock.cpp`.
6. In a protocol wrapper, wire I/O deadlines, the poll sleeps between reads and drain windows stay on `std::chrono::steady_clock`. A time stamp or deadline the wrapper hands to a driver's link health (link-loss, last-alive, a silence deadline checked with `check_silence`) is not a wire deadline: it reads the driver's `TaskClock` (rule 4), and only a wrapper that stamps one needs the clock. The clock comes from the driver, through `set_task_clock` (the OnStep wrapper, #1429, and the SkyWatcher wrapper) or the wrapper constructor (the three WandererAstro wrappers, #730). A wrapper that keeps a plain `link_lost_` flag and stamps nothing takes no clock. Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_protocol_wrapper.cpp`. Checked by: review.

## Alternatives rejected

Keep wall-clock waits and shorten the test timeouts: that trades a slow suite for a flaky one, which #535 already is. A per-driver clock parameter or template: the `task_wait_for` bodies would stay one copy per driver and each driver's tests would build their own fake. Put pointing time on the same clock: pointing time is wall time with a client-offset policy and a step detector, which is a different contract from a monotonic deadline; merging them would reopen decision 0001. Wrappers take the clock for every wait, or a clock seam in the transport: rejected for the reason in the Decision (the kernel read timeout stays real time, so a fake clock stalls the wire loop).

## Consequences

The [Adoption table](../adoption/0005-task-clock.md) is the current roster; this paragraph is the history of the moves. Drivers move onto the clock one at a time, starting with the SkyWatcher, Celestron and SynScan telescope drivers that share `task_wait_for`; the iOptron telescope driver followed (#1223): its slew poll and settle sleeps, deadlines and override windows read the clock, and its clock-sync body waits through it; the ToupTek camera and filter wheel drivers followed (#1268): the camera's pulse-guide and thermal-poll waits, exposure deadline and thermal cache ages, and the wheel's homing poll sleep read the clock; the Player One camera driver followed (#1279): its pulse-guide timer, exposure deadline and image-readiness poll read the clock; the QHY camera driver followed (#1386): its exposure watchdog, pulse-guide timer, settle sleep, worker waits and 2 s reap loops read the clock, and the QHY filter wheel's own settle-clock function became the same `TaskClock`; the Bisque telescope (#1162), the SVBONY and gphoto cameras (#1282) and the ZWO camera (#1328) followed; the Astroasis focuser, the Gemini focuser and flat panel, the QHY Q-Focuser and the Celestron focus motor (#1320) were on the clock from their first slot; the WandererAstro cover, filter wheel and box read it for link-health timing (#730), and the Gemini PDH switch for its link-fault stamp (#1987); the OnStep telescope moved with its first slot, the MoveAxis re-send (#1378): its status and position cache ages, slew grace, pulse-guide end, synchronous-slew poll and settle sleeps, re-send due times and the elapsed time since a UTCDate write read the clock; the Celestron CGX telescope's continuity deadline and settle sleeps moved in #1404, and its UTC anchor elapsed time and host-discipline re-check followed in #1983; a driver not yet moved keeps its raw sleeps, and the rule above applies only from the move on. The fake's wakeup rule needs a ThreadSanitizer case for the window between predicate check and block. The async operation slot and the state snapshot, which come later, test through this clock rather than through sleeps.
