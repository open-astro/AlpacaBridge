# Async operation ownership

Status: accepted

## Context

The Sky-Watcher, Celestron and SynScan telescope drivers each hand-roll the same background-task machinery. `task_wait_for` (in `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/skywatcher/skywatcher_telescope_driver.cpp`, `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/celestron/celestron_telescope_driver.cpp` and `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/synscan/synscan_telescope_driver.cpp`) is a condition-variable wait on `task_mutex_` with a cancel-flag predicate. `reap_slew_task`, in all three files, sets the cancel flag, notifies, moves the thread out under `task_mutex_` and joins it with no bound. Every new slew, park or home calls it on the HTTP thread, so a new command waits for the whole of the old body, including a serial transaction that is blocked until its own timeout. Sky-Watcher also has a per-axis `reap_stop_task(int axis)` and a `MoveAxis(axis, 0)` stop-completion body that checks the driver-wide `motion_generation_` at its tail, so that a slew, park, home or pulse that took over while it polled makes it skip its tracking restore. That check is the only place in the tree that tells a cancelled body from a superseded one, the distinction [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md) names.

## Decision

A driver's cancellable background bodies run in `util::AsyncOperation` slots (`AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/async_operation.h`), one body at a time per slot, and every wait in a slot goes through the injected `TaskClock` of [decision 0005](0005-task-clock.md).

`start()` does its capacity check, the move of the current body into the stale list, the generation bump, the thread creation and the assignment of the new body under the slot's one mutex, with no `joinable()` pre-check outside it. It does not wait for the body it replaces: it marks it superseded, wakes it and moves it to the stale list, joining only stale bodies that have already returned. At most three replaced bodies may still be running; a start that would make that four first waits on the clock for a stale body to return, for at most 2 s (the bounded-join budget, under ConformU's roughly 5 s timeout; ASCOM itself sets no per-call limit), and otherwise refuses with `InvalidOperation` and changes nothing: the current body is not superseded, the generation is not bumped and the kept failure is not cleared. That is the only wait `start()` makes.

Every start bumps an `OperationGeneration` that several slots may share (a slew slot and per-axis stop slots), and a driver may bump it directly for a motion command that runs without a slot. Which slots share one is the driver's choice; the slot knows nothing about axes. A body's stop reason is superseded when it was replaced on its own slot or the shared generation has moved past its token, otherwise cancelled when `cancel()` or `cancel_all_and_join()` reached it, otherwise none. Superseded takes precedence over cancelled whenever both apply, and the reason is sticky.

A body's `wait_for(d)` waits on the slot mutex and condition variable through the clock and returns whether the body may continue, evaluated under that mutex, so a cancel at the deadline wins. A cancel or a replacement on the body's own slot wakes it at once; a generation bump elsewhere does not, and the body sees it when its wait returns. `cancel()` marks and wakes the current body, never joins and never blocks. `cancel_all_and_join()` and the destructor cancel the current body, wake every body and join every thread with no time bound; the slot never detaches a thread, and bounding a body that blocks in a call with no timeout of its own stays the caller's job. A throw from the body the slot started last is kept as the last failure until the next successful start, including when `cancel_all_and_join()` took that body and no start followed. A throw from a stale body, one replaced by a later start or taken by `cancel_all_and_join()` before a later start, is logged with `ALPACA_LOG_WARN` and never written to the last failure, so it cannot overwrite what the later start cleared, whatever order the throw and the start happen in. No throw reaches `std::terminate`.

Lock order is driver `mutex_`, then the slot mutex, and the slot runs no body, log call or user callback under its mutex. `start()` and `cancel_all_and_join()` are called without the driver mutex held, since a stale body may need it to return; `cancel()`, `running()`, `stale_count()` and `last_failure()` may be called with it held. The slot is neither copyable nor movable.

## Rules

The rules a change is checked against. Each names where it applies and what checks it; the reasoning is in the Decision above.

1. A driver runs each cancellable background body in a `util::AsyncOperation` slot and holds no raw `std::thread` member for one. Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/**`, `AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/vendor/**`. Checked by: review; the roster by `scripts/check_docs_drift.py` check 23.
2. `start()` never waits for the body it replaces, except the bounded wait at three stale bodies, and a refused start changes nothing. Scope: `AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/async_operation.h`. Checked by: `AlpacaCore/tests/test_async_operation.cpp`.
3. A new motion command never cancels and joins the running body before it starts its own: it supersedes it (bumping the generation under the driver `mutex_` before it sets its flags) or refuses with `InvalidOperation`. Cancel and join belongs to stop paths (AbortSlew, Halt, disconnect). Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_driver.cpp`. Checked by: review.
4. `start()` and `cancel_all_and_join()` are called without the driver `mutex_` held. Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_driver.cpp`. Checked by: review.
5. No body calls `start()` or `cancel_all_and_join()` on its own slot. Scope: `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_driver.cpp`. Checked by: review.
6. Superseded wins over cancelled, and a superseded body touches neither hardware nor driver state. Scope: `AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/async_operation.h`, `AlpacaCore/src/vendors/*/*_driver.cpp`. Checked by: `AlpacaCore/tests/test_async_operation.cpp`, review.

## Alternatives rejected

Join the old body on start, today's `reap_slew_task` shape: it blocks the HTTP thread for as long as a blocked transaction lasts. Detach the old body: AGENTS.md forbids detaching a thread that touches `this`. An unbounded stale list: a hung link would grow threads without limit, one per client retry. Waking bodies across slots on a generation bump: one slot would have to take another slot's mutex, which needs a cross-slot lock order that nothing else requires. Cancelled winning over superseded: a superseded body would send a stop to axes a newer operation owns.

## Consequences

The Adoption table below is the current roster; this paragraph is the history of the slices. The Sky-Watcher telescope is the pilot (slice K2): its slew, goto, Park and FindHome bodies run in one slot, and each axis's `MoveAxis(axis, 0)` stop-completion body in a slot of its own; its `slew_task_thread_`, `stop_task_thread_[2]`, `reap_slew_task` and `reap_stop_task` are gone. Celestron (slice K4) and SynScan (slice K5) follow, and each slice deletes that driver's `task_wait_for` and `reap_*` bodies. Slice K3 moved the Sky-Watcher pulse guide (one slot per axis, each with a generation of its own) and the one-shot rate-applied check (a slot of its own) onto slots, so `pulse_task_thread_[2]`, `pulse_task_cancel_[2]`, `rate_verify_thread_` and their reap code are gone; slice K3b moved the sub-floor duty-cycle worker (both axes, 50 ms tick) onto a slot of its own as one long-lived periodic body, so `duty_thread_`, `duty_lifecycle_mutex_` and `reap_duty_task` are gone; the setter-driven restart and the deferred-write pre-arm stay in the driver. Slice K4 moved the Celestron async slew and Park bodies onto one slew slot and the pulse-guide chunk chain onto one slot per axis (each with a generation of its own), so `slew_task_thread_`, `pulse_task_threads_[2]`, their cancel flags, `task_mutex_`, `task_wait_for`, `reap_slew_task` and `reap_pulse_task(s)` are gone and no raw `std::thread` member remains. Celestron keeps its cancel-and-join handoff: every initiator cancels and joins the previous body before it publishes its own flags (the old body's tail would otherwise write over them), so a Celestron body sees `Cancelled`, never `Superseded`. This does not meet the Decision (it is the rejected join-on-start shape) and is tracked as a defect in #1406. A refused `start()` after a body was cancelled stops the mount and publishes the AbortSlew state (`start_slew_body()`); a refusal that cancelled nothing restores the flags and sends no stop. Celestron `pulse_guide()` joins and starts its pulse slot with no driver mutex held, and a refused pulse start stops the axis it had started. Slice K5 did the same for SynScan (`slew_task_thread_`, `pulse_task_threads_[2]`, the cancel flags, `task_mutex_`, `task_wait_for` and the `reap_*` helpers are gone), including the refused-start stop; its pulse stop body takes `mutex_` (after an RA stop, to restore tracking, and on failure), so `pulse_guide()` cancels and joins it with no driver mutex held and a refused pulse start stops the axis it had started. SynScan also kept the Celestron cancel-and-join handoff for its initiators; that is tracked as a defect in #1407. The Celestron and SynScan Park flows share `util::run_park_slew()` and `util::stop_goto_and_axes()` (`AlpacaCore/include/alpacacore/util/park_slew.h`); each driver keeps its target, wire commands and the state it clears in hooks. The Sky-Watcher rig ConformU run this record waited for passed on 2026-10-11: the EQM-35 Pro, ConformU 4.5.1, 0 errors, 0 issues and 0 timing issues (`AlpacaCore/conformu/SkyWatcher/EQM-35 Pro/Linux-arm64.txt`), on a build of the #1046 branch, every commit of which carries the slots. The slots are on `main` only: `stable/5.0` and every 5.0.0 beta keep the hand-rolled threads, so a ConformU run on a 5.0 build does not exercise them, and they first ship in the next release cut from `main` after 5.0. The utility is tested on the fake clock only (`AlpacaCore/tests/test_async_operation.cpp`), with a `[stress-guard]` double-start storm under ThreadSanitizer.

What the pilot settled:

- **Bound.** `kStaleReapTimeout` stays 2 s. `start()` waits only when three replaced bodies are still running, so it takes four starts in a row, each replacing a body stuck in one transaction on the same link. The protocol wrapper serialises transactions, so those bodies drain one per transaction: at most 1 s on serial (`response_timeout_ms`, 250 ms for the retransmitted stop), at most about 3 s on UDP (three 1 s attempts, `kUdpRetries`). A client that fires four initiators inside one blocked transaction is not ConformU's pattern, and on that path a refusal (`InvalidOperation`) after 2 s is the intended answer; the 1 s STANDARD target applies to the healthy path, where `start()` never waits.
- **Generations.** The slew slot has its own generation; each axis's stop slot has one of its own. Stop slots do not share one because a stop on axis 1 would mark an in-flight axis-0 stop body Superseded and skip its tracking restore. The same-axis refinement near the stop body's tail stays on `motion_generation_`, which the slot does not know.
- **Claim.** A start does not wait for the body it replaces, so the initiator bumps the slot's generation under the driver mutex before it sets the new operation's flags. A replaced body checks, under that mutex, that it is not Superseded before it touches a flag or the axes; without the claim it could clear a flag the new operation had just set.
- **Thread refusal.** The slot wraps it. `start()` creates the thread first, before any bookkeeping, and turns a `std::system_error` (EAGAIN) into `AlpacaException(DriverException)`; the current body, the generation and the kept failure are unchanged. That is true of the slot only: a driver initiator that claimed the Sky-Watcher slew state (`claim_slew_slot_locked()`) before the refused start leaves the previously current body superseded, so it skips its tracking restore and stop; when that claim replaced a body in flight, the initiator therefore stops both axes itself and publishes the AbortSlew state (`start_slew_body()`), then throws; a refusal that replaced nothing cancels nothing. Every driver gets the same mapping from one place.
- **`cancel()` never reaps.** Unchanged: up to three finished but unjoined bodies wait for the next `start()` or for disconnect, a bounded number.
- **Re-entrancy.** No body calls `start()` or `cancel_all_and_join()` on its own slot: the second joins its own thread, the first can wait out `kStaleReapTimeout`. A body uses `cancel()` or `ctx.stop_reason()`.
- **Pulse and rate-check slots (K3).** Each axis's pulse slot has its own generation, shared with neither the slew slot nor the other axis: a pulse start would otherwise supersede a running slew body or the other axis's pulse. A slew, park, home, abort, sync, MoveAxis or disconnect ends a pulse by cancelling and joining its slot with no driver mutex held, as the old reap did, so a pulse that stops its axis late cannot run after the new command; the `initiator_mutex_` that `pulse_guide()` already takes keeps its join and start from interleaving with a second pulse. The rate-applied check has a slot of its own (sharing the RA pulse slot would let a pulse start supersede a pending check the old code kept), and it is ended with the driver mutex held, so it can only `cancel()`: its resend takes the driver mutex and goes out only while the epoch it started with is current. The pulse body's rate checks resend the same way, gated on the slot context.
- **Kept failure.** The Sky-Watcher keeps `last_slew_error_` rather than the slot's last failure, since AbortSlew, MoveAxis and a reconnect clear it without starting a body.
