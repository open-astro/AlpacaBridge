### Breaking changes
- **ZWO AM, OnStep, Celestron NexStar and SynScan no longer write an untrusted host clock into the mount** (ZWO, OnStep, Celestron and SynScan drivers, issue #2419): a connect with "Sync time on connect" on (the ZWO AM default) used to push the host clock into the mount even when the host had booted on a stale clock with no network time, which can put the mount hours out. It now writes the mount clock only once the host clock is trusted (network time synchronised, or a client UTCDate write or Sync Time set it, or the server saw it step), and logs one warning naming the mount clock it left alone. The ZWO AM also skips its time write before a GOTO while the host clock is untrusted. The site still goes out. **After upgrading:** on a host without network time, send the time from your app (for example NINA's time sync, which writes UTCDate) or press Sync Time in the web UI; the mount clock is then written as before, except that a star-aligned Celestron NexStar or SynScan hand controller keeps its own clock until a client UTCDate write or a reconnect.

### Fixed
- **ZWO AM, OnStep, Celestron NexStar and SynScan re-sync the mount clock when the host clock is set or steps** (ZWO, OnStep, Celestron and SynScan drivers, issue #2419): with "Sync time on connect" on, a connected mount now gets the host clock again when the host clock becomes trusted or steps, on its own background slot, as the iOptron driver already did. The re-sync waits while a GOTO, Park, FindHome, MoveAxis or PulseGuide is under way (checked again inside its own write), gives up after 15 minutes of motion with one warning and no write, and lets a client UTCDate write made meanwhile win. A disconnect ends a waiting re-sync, and a reconnect does not inherit one. On a Celestron NexStar or SynScan hand controller that is star-aligned, the re-sync leaves the clock alone, because a clock change would shift its pointing model; the connect and a client UTCDate write still set it. For saved setups on an untrusted host, see Breaking changes.
- **iOptron host clock re-sync edge cases** (iOptron driver, issue #2419): a host clock change that arrived just as a connect or disconnect returned could be lost; it is now stored before the driver tries the lock and drained after the lock is released. A client UTCDate write made while the re-sync waited out a GOTO could be overwritten by the re-sync; the re-sync now checks for a newer client write inside its own `:SUT` exchange and sends nothing. The re-sync's motion wait now gives up after 15 minutes, like the other mounts. The time conversions on the clock write path are now thread safe (`gmtime_r`/`localtime_r`), as are OnStep's and SynScan's.
- **ZWO AM: SiderealTime and the pier sides follow the mount clock** (ZWO driver, issue #2419): `SiderealTime`, `SideOfPier` and `DestinationSideOfPier` now take local sidereal time from the mount's clock (the UTCDate anchor: a time write, or the `:GMTI` read at connect and every 30 s) once it is known, and from the host clock only before that. They then agree with UTCDate and with the RA the mount reports by its own clock, also when an untrusted connect left a mount clock that differs from the host clock. On a host with network time the two clocks agree and the answers do not change.
- **Celestron NexStar: a failed or timed-out blocking SlewToCoordinates now stops the mount and keeps its error** (Celestron driver): a blocking `SlewToCoordinates` whose GOTO dispatch throws after it started, or whose 120 s wait times out, used to throw and leave the mount moving with Slewing still true. It now sends the GOTO and axis stops, as a failed asynchronous slew already did, and Slewing raises the failure until the next motion command or a reconnect. A stop that fails is logged and the error says the mount may still be moving. The stale Slewing flag it left used to hold a later clock re-sync for its whole 15-minute bound.

### Added (tests)
- **Mount host clock trust and re-sync cases** (issue #2419): `test_zwo_mount_clock_trust.cpp`, `test_onstep_clock_trust.cpp`, `test_celestron_clock_trust.cpp` and `test_synscan_clock_trust.cpp`, plus new cases in `test_ioptron_clock_sync_slot.cpp`. Existing ZWO, OnStep and SynScan cases whose connect must write the clock now hold the host clock trusted, so they pass on a runner without network time.
