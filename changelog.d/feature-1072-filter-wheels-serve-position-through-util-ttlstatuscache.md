### Changed

- **ZWO EFW, Player One Phoenix and ToupTek AFW filter wheels answer `Position` from a 100 ms status frame, and their connect, close and moves no longer block `Names`**, filter wheel drivers, issue #1072 (the iOptron iEFW follows in its own change). Two `Position` reads inside 100 ms cost one SDK call; a read after `set_position` goes to the device, also when the move throws; `-1` while the wheel moves comes through unchanged and is never held; three consecutive failed reads latch `<wheel> communications compromised` and a good read clears it; a reconnect drops the cached frame and the failure count; `get_names` and `get_name` return within 150 ms while a `Position` read or a connect is held. The ToupTek frame ages on the driver's task clock, so `FakeTaskClock::advance()` expires it (`util::TtlStatusCache` takes an optional `TaskClock`, the real clock by default). A non-Alpaca SDK failure in a Player One `Position` read now surfaces as a `DriverException`. Each wheel takes the decision 0016 lock order (`transition_mutex_`, cache, `io_mutex_`, `mutex_`, SDK). The ToupTek homing poll still reads the SDK directly, and a disconnect that lands as the homing ends is honoured (the wheel stays disconnected). A connect that fails leaves the ZWO and Player One wheels with their pre-connect name and slot limits. Covered by the `[position-cache]` cases in `AlpacaCore/tests/test_zwo_seam_fakes.cpp`, `test_playerone_filterwheel.cpp` and `test_touptek_filterwheel.cpp` (the clock case is "the Position TTL follows the injected task clock").
