### Fixed
- **OnStep, Celestron and SynScan mounts no longer report a stop that failed as stopped** (AlpacaCore, issue #742; `onstep_telescope_driver.cpp`, `celestron_telescope_driver.cpp`, `synscan_telescope_driver.cpp`): six stop commands sat in `catch (...) {}`, and the driver then reported the mount as stopped. A stop fails when the link is bad (a serial dropout, Wi-Fi loss, a busy handset), and `poll_hardware_slewing_locked()` keeps the cached `Slewing` value when its own poll fails, so a mount still moving read `Slewing` false, which is what a client's safety logic acts on. OnStep `MoveAxis(axis, 0)` now tries both direction stops and, if either fails, throws `DriverException` ("MoveAxis stop failed: ...") and leaves the axis reported as moving. On Celestron and SynScan the cancel-GOTO and the two axis stops are each tried on their own, so one failure no longer skips the others; when the park task fails (a dispatch failure or the 120 s park timeout) and a stop fails, an ERROR line "stop after park failure failed: ...; the mount may still be moving" is logged and `Slewing` keeps its cached value, and `Unpark` during a park still clears the park state and joins the park task, then throws `DriverException` ("Unpark could not stop the park slew: ...") instead of returning success. When every stop is answered nothing changes. Hardware-free cases in `test_onstep_telescope.cpp`, `test_celestron_async_park.cpp` and `test_synscan_async_park.cpp` cover each path; the OnStep and SynScan stops are blind sends that an unanswering fake cannot fail, so `FakeMountServer` gained `drop_connections()`, which resets the live connections from the mount's side (a zero-linger close sends the RST itself, so the next stop fails without depending on a race). ConformU not re-run.
