### Fixed

- **OnStep: a `MoveAxis` whose re-send cannot start is stopped and reported** (issue #1458): the driver keeps a `MoveAxis` going by re-sending it every 7 s from a background task (#1378). If that task could not start after the move went out (no thread, or earlier tasks still finishing), `MoveAxis` failed while the mount kept moving until the firmware's time limit and `Slewing` read true. It now stops that axis and throws `DriverException`. The re-send no longer reads `:GU#` every 7 s for a move on firmware that never reports it (issue #1461), and the wrapper forgets its link-loss stamps when the driver's clock changes, so a loss stamped on one driver's clock is not timed on the next driver's (issue #1460, tests only). Two re-send paths that had no test are now covered: a stop that cannot be sent still ends the re-send, and a `MoveAxis` that cannot be sent leaves the other axis's re-send running (issue #1459). The connect no longer holds the firmware lock across its version reads, so the firmware hook never waits for the serial port (decision 0016). Tests: `test_onstep_telescope.cpp` "OnStep Telescope Driver - a MoveAxis whose re-send cannot start stops the axis and fails", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a MoveAxis stop that cannot be sent still ends the re-send", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a MoveAxis that cannot be sent ends its axis's earlier re-send", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a refused re-send whose stop fails too leaves Slewing true", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a refused re-send stops nothing after a newer MoveAxis", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a refused re-send stops nothing after an AbortSlew", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a pulse guide at the first re-send tick does not end the re-send", "OnStep Telescope Driver - a MoveAxis that cannot be sent leaves the other axis's re-send running" and "OnStep Telescope Driver - a MoveAxis the mount does not show is never re-sent", "OnStep Telescope Driver - the firmware hook does not wait for the connect's version reads"; `test_onstep_link_loss.cpp` "OnStep link loss - a loss stamped on one driver's clock is not timed on the next driver's", "OnStep link loss - a change of clock forgets the wrapper's loss stamps" and "OnStep link loss - a driver that does not own the clock leaves the loss stamps alone".
